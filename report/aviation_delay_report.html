Where airlines lose time: carrier-attributable delay in US domestic flights¶

This analysis uses US DOT (BTS) flight data rather than Singapore data, since CAAS/data.gov.sg only publishes aggregate monthly figures for Changi, and no flight-level, cause-coded delay data is publicly available. BTS was chosen as the best available public dataset with the granularity needed for this kind of analysis.

Question. Which airports or carriers show disproportionately high carrier-attributable delay, and does time-of-day or day-of-week make it worse?

What "carrier-attributable" means. BTS splits each delay of 15+ minutes into five causes. Carrier delay covers causes inside the airline's control: maintenance, crew, cleaning, fuelling, baggage. BTS has no separate "mechanical" field, so this is not a measure of mechanical problems. Weather, air-traffic-system (NAS) and security delays sit outside the airline's control. Late-arriving-aircraft delay is knock-on delay from an earlier flight and is kept separate.

Data: Jan 2024 – Dec 2025, 13,844,730 completed flights. Carrier-attributable delay averages 5.2 min per flight and is 33% of all cause-coded delay minutes.

Summary¶

SkyWest (OO) and American (AA) have disproportionately high carrier-attributable delay: 8.0 and 7.2 minutes per flight, against an all-airline average of 5.2. They're at least 1.3× the rate of other airlines flying from the same airports, and at least 1.3× the average in 2024 and 2025 separately.

Time of day makes it worse. Across all airlines, carrier-attributable delay per flight is 2.0× higher at 22:00 than at 10:00, and Sunday is the worst day of the week. But for both flagged carriers the biggest excess is in the first wave: departures scheduled before 09:00.

Recommendation: SkyWest (OO) and American (AA) should fix the delays on their first flights of the day, the ones scheduled before 09:00. Bringing those down to the all-airline pre-09:00 rate would remove about 20,600 carrier-attributable delay hours a year. That is before counting the knock-on delay those early flights pass to the rest of the day.

1 · Carriers¶

Finding 1 · Carriers. Two airlines meet every test:

  • SkyWest (OO): 8.0 min per flight, 1.54× the average; 1.34× after controlling for airports flown; 2024: 1.51×, 2025: 1.56×. 36,500 excess delay hours a year.
  • American (AA): 7.2 min per flight, 1.38× the average; 1.33× after controlling for airports flown; 2024: 1.43×, 2025: 1.32×. 31,600 excess delay hours a year.

They fail in different ways. When a carrier-caused delay happens, the typical one lasts 35 min at SkyWest and 17 min at American (all airlines: 19 min). They also happen on 11% and 15% of flights (all airlines: 12%). So SkyWest's delays are longer, and American's are more frequent.

Above 1.2× overall but not in every year or after the mix control: JetBlue (B6), Allegiant (G4) and Frontier (F9). For example, JetBlue's rate fell from 1.54× in 2024 to 1.02× in 2025.

2 · Airports¶

Grouped by origin airport: where the delayed flight was due to leave from.

Finding 2 · Airports. 3 of 102 major airports meet every test:

  • Detroit, MI (DTW): 7.5 min per departure (1.44×; 1.25× against the same airlines elsewhere). 250,604 departures; main operators Delta (DL) 47%, SkyWest (OO) 21%.
  • Madison, WI (MSN): 7.3 min per departure (1.40×; 1.26× against the same airlines elsewhere). 24,110 departures; main operators SkyWest (OO) 21%, American (AA) 17%.
  • Des Moines, IA (DSM): 6.7 min per departure (1.29×; 1.27× against the same airlines elsewhere). 29,443 departures; main operators American (AA) 15%, United (UA) 14%.

Airports matter less than carriers. The airport effect is smaller once airline mix is controlled for, and the flagged airports add up to 5,600 excess hours a year, against 68,100 for the flagged carriers.

3 · Time of day and day of week¶

By scheduled departure hour (local time), so a late flight doesn't move into a later hour.

Finding 3 · Time of day and day of week. Across all airlines, carrier-attributable delay is lowest in the late morning (3.9 min per flight at 10:00) and climbs through the afternoon and evening to 8.0 min at 22:00. Sunday (6.0 min) is the worst day and Wednesday (4.5) the best. The weekday effect is smaller than the hour-of-day effect.

The flagged carriers follow the same shape but sit above the average at every hour, and their gap is widest early in the day, when airlines as a whole run better than their daily average. Before 09:00 they run 1.76× and 1.51× the all-airline rate. That band is 21% and 26% of their flights but 28% and 33% of their excess delay hours. Most of these flights start the day from an overnight position. That points at readiness (maintenance sign-off, crew, aircraft preparation), though the BTS data can't say which.

Recommendation¶

Fix the first wave at SkyWest (OO) and American (AA). Their pre-09:00 departures run 1.76× and 1.51× the all-airline carrier-attributable delay rate. Bringing them to the all-airline rate would remove about 20,600 delay hours a year (SkyWest (OO) 10,300, American (AA) 10,300). Likely places to look, which BTS can't separate: overnight maintenance sign-off, crew show-up and aircraft readiness (cleaning, catering, fuelling) at the airports where their first flights of the day leave from. All of these are inside the airline's control, which is what this metric measures.

The number is conservative. A late first departure pushes delay onto later flights, and BTS books that as late-arriving aircraft delay (40% of all recorded delay minutes), which is not counted here.

Caveats¶

  • Carrier delay ≠ mechanical delay. BTS records maintenance in the same field as crew, cleaning, fuelling and baggage.
  • Short delays aren't counted. Delay causes are only recorded for flights 15+ minutes late.
  • Knock-on delay is left out. Late-arriving-aircraft delay is partly the carrier's own knock-on delay, but BTS doesn't record what caused it. Leaving it out of "carrier-attributable" makes the estimate conservative.
  • SkyWest is a regional carrier. It flies under the United, Delta, American and Alaska brands but operates and reports these flights itself. Its delays are its own operation's, not the brand's.
  • The CrowdStrike outage doesn't change the main finding. The IT outage of 19 July 2024 hit Delta hardest. With 19–28 July 2024 removed, SkyWest and American stay flagged (see appendix). Frontier, just under the bar in 2024 (1.17×), also crosses it (1.22×), because the outage week pushed the all-airline average up.
  • Delay causes are self-reported by airlines. BTS covers US domestic flights by carriers above a revenue threshold, so the results may not carry over to other markets such as Changi.
